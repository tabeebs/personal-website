    a placeholder piece about nothing in particular

    A short line that says what the piece is about, in one breath.

    contents

      This is placeholder text, standing in for the opening of a piece. It's here so the page has the rhythm of real writing: a few lines, a pause, then the next thought. Nothing here means anything yet.

      A second paragraph, a little longer than the first, so you can see how body text sits against the background colour, how wide the column feels, and how far apart the paragraphs are. Read it the way you'd read something real.

      where it starts

      Every section begins with a heading like the one above. The rail on the left picks each one up on its own, so a piece only has to be written with headings to get the contents for free.

      Scroll slowly and watch the rail: the line follows you down, and the marker for the section you're in fills in. Hover the rail to see every section's name; click one and the line glides there with you.

      the first idea

      Placeholder for an idea worth a section of its own. It can run for a few paragraphs before the next heading comes along, and the rail doesn't mind how long a section is.

      A pulled-out line sits here, set apart from the rest with a thin bar, for the one sentence you'd want someone to remember.

      And then the writing carries on as before, with another paragraph that exists mostly to take up room and make the page long enough to scroll through properly.

      a smaller thought

      Smaller headings (h3s) show up in the rail too, tucked in a step, so a long section can be broken up without the rail getting crowded.

      and another

      One more small section, so the indent has company. A real piece might have none of these, or lots.

      something in the middle

      a figure, a diagram or an image could sit here

      Placeholder text again, after the figure. Long pieces need room to breathe around anything that isn't words, so the figure gets the same column as the text and a bit more space above and below.

      Another paragraph, for length. The point of all this is to feel how the page reads, not what it says, so read it the way you'd read something you actually cared about, and notice what gets in the way.

      two sides of it

      A section with two small parts under it, to show how a structured piece looks in the rail.

      one way to see it

      The first side, in a sentence or two of placeholder text. Then a second sentence, to make it a paragraph.

      the other way

      The second side, also placeholder. If this were real, this is where the piece would turn.

      where it ends

      The last section. When you reach the bottom of the page, the rail's line runs all the way down, so you can tell at a glance that you've finished.

      A closing line, short on purpose.
